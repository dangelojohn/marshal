# Venue Operations JTBD — the human job behind the software

Research date: 2026-08-07. Complements `06_venue_facility_platforms.md`, which covers *what software venues use*. This file covers *what operations/facilities staff actually do*, sourced from job postings, employee reviews, certification curricula, trade-association program listings, and one confirmed structural-failure case study — deliberately not vendor marketing pages, which is what file 06 already covers. Every claim tagged [SOURCED] (URL given) / [VENDOR] / [INDUSTRY-COMMON] / [UNVERIFIED] / [SPECULATIVE].

WebSearch hit its session budget after 8 queries; the remainder was done via direct WebFetch against known/guessed URLs and DuckDuckGo's HTML endpoint (same fallback file 06 used) — flagged wherever that matters.

---

## Executive summary

The clearest, most load-bearing finding is not a fact about tasks — it's a fact about where those tasks are documented at all. **Trade press does not describe operations.** A TSNN "Executive Interview" with the CEO of Music City Center, published under TSNN's own "operations leadership perspectives" framing, contained *zero* mentions of docks, move-in, labor, unions, or safety [SOURCED, tsnn.com/convention-center/executive-interview-series-charles-starks...]. The operational reality only shows up three places: (1) job postings, which reveal task lists because they're legally obligated to; (2) hourly-employee reviews on Indeed, unfiltered by PR; and (3) the union/trade jurisdiction rules a venue publishes for exhibitors, which read like contract law because they are.

This validates the project owner's own framing thesis directly — GES's "real JTBD is moving company" applies here too: **a convention centre's real JTBD is being a landlord who has subcontracted the physical work to a half-dozen unionized trades and now spends its operational headcount managing the boundaries between them** — not "hosting events" in the marketing sense.

## 1. Reconstructing a move-in day

No single source gave an hour-by-hour account — this section is a reconstruction assembled from multiple SOURCED fragments, stitched together. Where the account bridges a gap rather than quoting, it's marked [SPECULATIVE, built from SOURCED fragments].

- **Pre-dawn / early shift start**: San Diego Convention Center's Director of Event Operations posting describes the venue as a **"24/7/365 operation"** run across **"all three shifts,"** with **"daily and event-based scheduling, staffing, and task assignment across all three shifts"** [SOURCED, talents.vaia.com/companies/san-diego-convention-center-corporation/director-of-event-operations-24946088/]. Shift-based coverage means someone is always on for a move-in that starts before public hours — architecturally different from a 9-5 office, and the reason the senior ops role exists as a 24/7 staffing-and-changeover function first, not an event-planning function.
- **Trucks arrive, dock coordinator takes over**: Colorado Convention Center's **Dock/Conveyance Coordinator** role (via ASM Global/Legends) does **"internal oversight and monitoring of all facility loading dock areas"** plus **"coordinating with outside contractors and vendors,"** including **"monitoring conveyances to assist in the movement of materials and personnel"** and **"pre and post event walks of dock areas to track/report building damage"** [SOURCED via WebSearch snippet of teamworkonline.com/.../dock-conveyance-coordinator-2165252 — the live posting itself returned only a closed-job stub on direct fetch, so treat as a secondary snippet, not a primary-source screenshot]. Critically, this coordinator does **not** move freight — see §4.
- **Freight moves under union jurisdiction, not venue staff**: at McCormick Place, once trucks are at the dock, **"Teamsters"** handle **"the handling of all material (except machinery) in and out of the exhibit hall"** — exhibitors may hand-carry small items but **"Four wheel dollies are not permitted."** **"Decorators install signs and draping"** (exhibitors may hang their own signage only if it takes under 30 minutes and needs no tools/ladder). Separately, **"Carpenters, Electricians, Plumbers, Riggers, and Projectionists provide specialized labor services"** [SOURCED, mccormickplace.com/exhibitors/]. The actual mechanic of a move-in floor is a jurisdictional assembly line, not a single crew.
- **Exhibitors check in, don't just show up**: at McCormick Place exhibitors must **"proceed to the Exhibitor Service Center to check in with service contractors,"** and are warned an advance utility order **"does NOT mean that services are installed prior to your arrival"** [SOURCED, same page] — paying in advance buys a place in a queue, not a guaranteed timestamp, a direct expression of the turnaround-capacity constraint file 06 already identified from the software side.
- **Facilities/venue-readiness layer runs in parallel**: the Director of Event Operations role also owns **"venue readiness for all events"** and **"managing complex changeovers"** while coordinating with **"Engineering and Capital Projects"** and **"contracted third-party labor providers"** [SOURCED, same SDCC posting] — building-systems readiness (HVAC, power, life-safety) is a distinct thread running alongside the freight/labor thread, not downstream of it.
- **Physical toll, real-time, on hourly staff**: Indeed reviews (Glassdoor returned 403 on every attempt this session) describe the literal texture: a Javits Center housekeeper wrote **"No time for resting u need to walk all the time manager will be at ur back all the time"**; a McCormick Place review described work areas as **"very cold like a meat cooler"** and noted **"hours of schedule change often based on need"**; an Orange County Convention Center Electrical Foreman (20-year tenure) described historically **"plenty of overtime guaranteed"** but noted COVID cut hours; another OCCC reviewer described catwalk work at height with **"Learned by myself, no training; but they expect you know everything"** [all SOURCED via WebFetch summarization of live Indeed review pages — flagged because the fetch tool summarizes rather than returns raw HTML, so treat as high-fidelity paraphrase except where quotation marks appear].
- **Post-event, the loop closes on liability, not celebration**: the dock coordinator's **post**-event walk exists specifically to "track/report building damage" [SOURCED, as above] — the operational close of a move-out is a damage audit, because the venue's real financial exposure in a turnaround cycle is property damage and jurisdictional grievances, not whether the show "went well."

## 2. Core operational competencies, ranked by evidence of daily attention/headcount

Ranked by how much job-posting/curriculum/staffing evidence each competency generated — not by how important it sounds in marketing:

1. **Space/turnaround & changeover staffing management** — the single most senior, most headcount-visible function found. SDCC's Director of Event Operations exists specifically to run **"daily and event-based scheduling, staffing, and task assignment across all three shifts"** of a 24/7/365 operation and to manage **"complex changeovers"** [SOURCED, as above]. A management-of-people-and-time problem first, an events problem second.
2. **Dock/freight coordination via union trade jurisdiction** — evidenced by (a) a dedicated Dock/Conveyance Coordinator title existing at all, (b) the marshalling yard being a physically named, mapped zone (**"Marshaling Yard"** with "easy same-level docking stations" and "underground access to Exhibit Hall A" at Minneapolis Convention Center) [SOURCED, minneapolis.org/minneapolis-convention-center/event-planners/floor-plans/level-one/marshaling-yard/], and (c) McCormick Place's explicit, granular trade-jurisdiction rules (§1). A coordination-and-compliance function, not a hands-on-freight function — the coordinator watches, logs, and schedules; Teamsters touch the material.
3. **Union labor liaison / collective bargaining compliance** — an explicit, non-optional hiring qualification, not folded into #1: SDCC requires **"Knowledge of collective bargaining agreements and union collaboration"** and lists HR as a named coordination partner **"regarding labor relations and union matters"** [SOURCED]; a DC Convention Center Director of Operations posting (Aramark) separately requires **"Prior Union property experience required"** as a standalone qualification line [SOURCED, aramarkcareers.com/.../Director-of-Operations-DC-Convention-Center-DC-20001/1368345800/]. Graded as its own competency because it governs *who is allowed to do what*, independent of scheduling or dock logistics.
4. **Safety/compliance** — present in every posting found but structurally diffused rather than headcount-concentrated: SDCC requires **"OSHA training or similar certifications... preferred"** and frames safety as **"a shared responsibility"** rather than a dedicated department [SOURCED]; McCormick Place's Aramark-contracted Facilities Operations Manager posting names **"Quality, Environmental & Health and Safety Compliance"** as one line among many management duties, not a standalone role [SOURCED, aramarkcareers.com/.../Facilities-Operations-Manager-McCormick-Place-IL-60616/1306377300/]. No dedicated "Director of Safety" title surfaced anywhere — safety reads as a compliance layer every manager owns, not a separate operational centre of gravity.
5. **Revenue protection via exclusivity (F&B/AV/rigging/telecom)** — real (McCormick Place: **"exclusive provider of Internet and Telecommunication services"**; catering run by OVG Hospitality [SOURCED]) but this research found no dedicated headcount or job title enforcing it day-to-day — a contracts/finance-office function layered on top of operations, not a floor-level daily-attention competency, which is why it's ranked last despite file 06 flagging it as financially central. Attention and revenue protection are not the same axis.

This ranking is a genuine finding, not a restatement of the brief: the CEM certification body implicitly agrees with roughly this ordering — its course list runs **Facilities & Site Selection → Floor Plan Development → Event Operations (logistics, on-site team management, show-rules enforcement) → Selecting Service Contractors (OSC/EAC roles) → Security, Risk & Crisis Management → Housing & Registration Management** [SOURCED, iaee.com/cem/cem-course-descriptions/] — logistics/on-site team management and contractor selection are named as distinct, core competency blocks; security/risk is present but positioned later, not first.

## 3. JTBD statements

1. **When move-in day arrives and multiple GSCs, AV companies, and rental companies converge on a limited number of dock doors and conveyances**, a Dock/Conveyance Coordinator wants to monitor and sequence access in real time and log dock-area condition before and after, **so they can** prevent gridlock, protect the venue against damage-liability claims, and avoid stalling crews who bill by the hour. [Colorado CC Dock/Conveyance Coordinator duty list, SOURCED above.]

2. **When a hall must flip from one show's teardown to the next show's move-in inside a fixed window**, a Director of Event Operations wants to staff and sequence all three shifts of a 24/7/365 operation as one orchestrated changeover, **so they can** hit contractual "venue readiness" without paying for shift overlap that doesn't actually close the gap. [SDCC posting's "managing complex changeovers" + three-shift 24/7/365 language, SOURCED above.]

3. **When any material needs to physically move in or out of an exhibit hall**, an on-site Operations Manager wants the correct union trade (Teamsters for freight, Decorators for signage/draping, Riggers for anything suspended or strapped) assigned before work starts, **so they can** stay inside jurisdictional agreements and avoid a grievance or work stoppage that halts the entire floor — not just the one booth. [McCormick Place's explicit trade-jurisdiction rules, SOURCED above.]

4. **When hiring or promoting into a senior operations role**, a venue's leadership wants a candidate who already understands collective bargaining agreements and can "collaborate" with organized labor on their own, **so they can** avoid HR/labor disputes derailing an event date that, unlike almost any other kind of deadline, cannot move. [SDCC posting's explicit CBA/union-collaboration requirement and the DC Convention Center Aramark posting's standalone "Prior Union property experience required" line, both SOURCED above.]

5. **When a marquee show creates a seasonal surge**, a Facilities supervisor wants to convert temporary/part-time labor into a workforce that can absorb the walking/standing/overtime load without breaking safety or building-damage procedures that are otherwise inconsistently enforced during normal operations, **so they can** get through the peak without an incident that becomes the more expensive problem. [San Diego Convention Center Indeed reviews describing Facilities overtime expectations and inconsistent procedures, SOURCED via indeed.com/cmp/San-Diego-Convention-Center-Corporation/reviews.]

## 4. The dock/marshalling yard as a physical daily-ops problem (venue side)

Directly complementary to file 06, which established that *no venue software vendor* names dock scheduling as a feature and that (before the correction landed in that file) it appeared served, if at all, by an outside point solution. Here's what the venue side actually looks like:

- **It is a named, mapped physical zone**, not an abstraction — Minneapolis Convention Center's official floor plans label a "Marshaling Yard" with same-level docking and underground access specifically to Exhibit Hall A [SOURCED, minneapolis.org, as above].
- **It is staffed by a coordinator, not a laborer** — where a dedicated role exists (Colorado CC's Dock/Conveyance Coordinator), the job is oversight, monitoring, damage documentation, and vendor coordination — not driving a forklift or unloading a truck [SOURCED, as above]. Physical handling is contractually reserved to a different party (Teamsters) at unionized venues.
- **Real-time decisions the coordinator actually makes**: which conveyance/dock door a given GSC/AV/rental-company truck gets and when (no dock-scheduling software confirmed anywhere in this research or file 06's for venues below the flagship tier — this is done by a human watching and coordinating); whether an item can be hand-carried by the exhibitor or must go through rigging labor (McCormick Place: anything needing tools to release restraining straps requires rigging labor) [SOURCED]; documenting building condition at the start and end of the event window to establish who's liable for what damage.
- **What goes wrong, per the evidence found**: (a) congestion, because no automated sequencing tool exists at most venues (file 06's finding, reaffirmed here from the operations side — the dock coordinator's "monitoring conveyances" duty exists precisely because nothing else is doing that sequencing); (b) building damage, serious enough to warrant a formal mandatory pre/post walk-and-log procedure as a named job duty, not an occasional check; (c) jurisdictional friction — rules like the four-wheel-dolly ban exist to protect labor jurisdiction, not (primarily) safety or throughput, meaning dock "efficiency" problems are sometimes actually labor-boundary problems wearing a logistics costume; (d) **safety incidents that essentially don't exist in public record** — OSHA's Establishment Search and Accident Search tools were run directly for "McCormick Place," "Javits Center," "convention center," and "trade show," returning **zero results on every query** [SOURCED attempt, osha.gov/ords/imis/ — flagged UNVERIFIED as an absence-finding: OSHA migrated off the legacy IMIS system, and this GET-based query interface may simply no longer execute searches server-side rather than genuinely returning "no incidents." Cannot distinguish "no incidents" from "tool broken" from this session's evidence]. The one confirmed, named structural failure found — the **David L. Lawrence Convention Center truss collapse, February 12, 2002, which killed one worker and injured two** [SOURCED, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_L._Lawrence_Convention_Center] — was a **construction-phase** failure (a 165-ton truss for the second building phase, not yet open) [SOURCED, same], not a show-day rigging or dock accident, so it illustrates convention centres as heavy-industrial construction sites more than routine move-in danger. General industry-wide forklift fatality statistics exist (OSHA-cited ~85 fatal forklift accidents/year nationally) [SOURCED via DuckDuckGo snippet, osha.gov/fatalities and forkliftquoteking.com] but no incident-specific search this session tied a forklift/dock/rigging fatality to a named convention centre.

## 5. Most surprising / non-obvious finding

**The dock's operating rules are shaped as much by protecting union jurisdiction as by physical safety or throughput.** McCormick Place bans exhibitors from using four-wheel dollies to hand-carry their own small items into their own booth — not because dollies are dangerous, but because unassisted material movement past a certain scale is Teamster-jurisdiction work, and the dolly ban is how that boundary gets enforced on the floor [SOURCED, mccormickplace.com/exhibitors/]. A generic warehouse dock-scheduling tool (Opendock, and the "JSDock" name file 06 now flags as likely non-existent) optimizes purely for throughput and would treat that rule as friction to be engineered away; it isn't friction, it's the actual operating system the venue runs on. **Any Marshal feature touching the dock or move-in floor needs to model who is contractually allowed to touch what, not just when trucks arrive** — that constraint is invisible from the software-vendor research in file 06 and invisible from generic freight-logistics thinking, and it only surfaced here because this pass read an exhibitor-facing operations page rather than a product page.

A close second, worth flagging because it validates the project owner's own research instinct: **trade press systematically omits operations even when explicitly billed as an operations profile.** TSNN's "Executive Interview Series" with Music City Center's CEO — a piece TSNN itself filed under operations-leadership framing — contained no mention of docks, labor, unions, or safety at all [SOURCED, tsnn.com, as above]. The operational reality of running a convention centre is present in job postings, union rulebooks, and hourly-worker reviews, and essentially absent from executive-facing trade coverage. That's the practical version of the GES example: polish is not evidence, and any future JTBD research on this industry should default to job postings/reviews/union documents over trade press, not the other way around.

## Honest gaps / what could not be found

- **WebSearch budget exhausted** after 8 queries this session — everything past that point came from direct WebFetch against known/guessed URLs or DuckDuckGo's HTML endpoint. Some categories (IAVM curriculum depth, IAEE beyond the course-title list, ESCA/EDPA safety-training content, "day in the life" trade profiles beyond the one TSNN piece) were not searched as broadly as the first few, and follow-up searches that thin results would normally justify weren't possible.
- **Glassdoor returned HTTP 403 on every direct fetch attempt** (5 tries) — all employee-review evidence in this file comes from Indeed instead, which loaded successfully.
- **IAVM** (International Association of Venue Managers — the actual body; a near-namesake "IAAM" doesn't appear to be it): confirmed it runs a Venue Management School, an Academy for Venue Safety & Security, VenueConnect, GuestX/Crowd Management Conference, and a Leadership Institute [SOURCED, iavm.org homepage], but every attempt to reach curriculum-detail pages (`/venue-management-school`, `/academy-venue-safety-security`, `/education`, `/education-training`, `/professional-development`) returned 404 — the org restructured its URLs and the correct paths couldn't be located without a working search budget. Curriculum content for IAVM's programs is an open gap.
- **ESCA and EDPA** (industry safety/labor associations): confirmed both reference labor and safety content (ESCA: a "Working with Labor" webinar, downloadable safety signage, a "Give Safety a Minute" blog series; EDPA: safety implied in its mission pillars) but neither's homepage yielded actual curriculum or training-content detail on direct fetch [SOURCED presence / UNVERIFIED detail, esca.org and edpa.com].
- **OSHA incident search**: as detailed in §4, the legacy IMIS-based search tools returned "no results" for every query attempted — cannot determine whether that reflects genuinely zero matching incidents or a broken/deprecated query interface. Treat as an open item, not a finding that convention-centre dock/forklift/rigging incidents don't happen.
- **BMO Centre Calgary and Vancouver Convention Centre** specifically did not surface dedicated job postings, reviews, or operations detail distinct from the U.S. venues covered — the U.S. venues (McCormick Place, Javits, San Diego, Orange County, Colorado, Minneapolis) carried this research, not the two Canadian flagship venues central to Marshal's own go-to-market.
