## 1. Map Your Show (MYS)

Booths can be locked "to prevent accidental changes, or reserve spaces for key exhibitors." Booths are color-coded by status/type. Includes an audit tool that flags "sizing or placement errors." Critically: **"Export functionality to DXF or PDF formats for general service contractors"** — i.e., the floor plan leaves the platform as a file, not a live feed.
- **Booth sales (MYS Sales Pro)** [SOURCED, mapyourshow.com/trade-show-booth-sales]: exhibitors "compare pricing of available spaces and self select their booth based on the available budget" — self-service, price/size-driven selection. Sponsorship inventory shown "in real time" alongside booths, single checkout. 15+ payment processors integrated. Customizable reporting dashboard for real-time booth-sales/sponsorship tracking. Explicitly supports on-site renewal: "leverage Booth Sales on-site to secure commitments from exhibitors for the following year."
- **Exhibitor management (Exhibitor Resource Center)** [SOURCED]: profile management, balance/invoice viewing, lead access, a deadline checklist with a "vendor connections" line item — this is a link out to the GSC's own portal, not a data integration (see Seam section).

- **Attendee/engagement layer (MYS Connected)** [SOURCED]: AI concierge ("Ask Lina"), matchmaking, wayfinding — a bolt-on product, separate from Floor Builder/Sales Pro.
- **Integrations confirmed**: CRM software, payment processors, AMS, registration [SOURCED].
- **Partners/GSC claim — corrected 2026-08-07, with an important nuance** [SOURCED, mapyourshow.com/partners]: the original "no GSC integration named anywhere" claim was checked directly and is **false as literally stated**. MYS runs an official "Our Partners" page, filterable by General Contractors / Registration / Event Management and Resellers, naming real GSCs: **Freeman, GES, Shepard, T3 Expo, Heritage, Fern, The Expo Group, Maritz, Showcare, Tradeshow Logic, Willwork Global Event Services, The Bridge Group, Evergreen, CM Event Solutions, American Tradeshow Services, TSE Live, MCI** — plus Registration partners Cvent, EventPass, Streampoint, and others (Event Advisory Group, PR Newswire, Fionta, Smith Bucklin, Momentive). ## The seam

Four parties, mapped by what system each one lives in day-to-day:

**(a) Show management platform** (MYS / a2z / ExpoCad / Cvent Exhibitor Management) — system of record for: booth inventory, booth-to-exhibitor assignment, exhibitor company/contact info, contract status, payment status, floor plan geometry.

**(b) General service contractor (GSC) ordering system** (GES's **Expresso**, Shepard's **ExhibitorPro**, Freeman's ordering portal — subdomain `fol.freemanco.com` confirmed to exist via the TLS certificate's alternate names returned when `freemanco.com` failed to load [SOURCED — this came from a certificate-mismatch error, not a marketing claim, so it's a harder data point than most in this document]) — system of record for: what services (carpet, electrical, furniture, labor, rigging, material handling) each exhibitor has ordered and owes for.

**(c) Venue/facility system** — building-level scheduling, dock/loading schedules, union labor jurisdiction, fire-marshal approval of the floor plan. Sometimes this is Momentus/Ungerboeck if the venue runs it; often it's a proprietary or paper-based process.

**(d) The exhibitor** — the one party forced to touch all of the above.

**Where the fragmentation is worst, with direct evidence:**

1. **Booth number does not travel from (a) to (b).** Fetching GES's live ordering portal (`ordering.ges.com`, product name **Expresso**) showed the exhibitor must search for their own booth by "Company Name, Booth Number, or Customer Number," with an explicit **"I Don't Know"** option next to the booth-number field, and a "Create One Here" fallback if the show/booth isn't already listed [SOURCED — this is the single strongest piece of direct evidence gathered this session]. That UI only makes sense if Expresso is **not** reading booth assignments live from whatever show-management platform the organizer used. The exhibitor — who already told the organizer their booth number when they signed the contract in MYS/a2z — has to re-supply it to the GSC from scratch, sometimes not even knowing it.

2. **The show-management platforms treat the GSC as a business partner to resell through, not a technical integration to build.** Map Your Show's own Exhibitor Resource Center checklist includes a "vendor connections" line item [SOURCED] — read in context, this is a pointer out to the GSC's separate portal, not a data feed. **Corrected 2026-08-07**: the original claim of "zero mentions of Freeman/GES/Shepard anywhere" across MYS's homepage, Floor Builder, Sales Pro, and integrations pages was checked further and is **false as literally stated** — MYS runs an official partners page (`mapyourshow.com/partners`) naming Freeman, GES, Shepard, and 14 other GSCs by name [SOURCED]. But the correction doesn't close the seam: that page is a **channel/reseller directory** ("The MYS Channel Partner Program extends our suite of services and the core platform to industry partners... allows partners to increase revenue opportunities by adding to their core offerings" [SOURCED]), and MYS's own Shepard-announcement blog post uses identical reseller/distribution language with zero mention of bidirectional data flow or booth-assignment sync [SOURCED, blog.mapyourshow.com]. Same absence-of-technical-integration result for a2z's exhibitor-management and floor-plan pages — not re-checked this pass. The only artifact that explicitly bridges organizer and GSC on any vendor page remains Floor Builder's **"export to DXF or PDF for general service contractors"** [SOURCED] — a one-way, point-in-time file drop, not a live sync. Booth moves made in the floor plan after that export require a fresh export and manual re-communication.

3. **ExpoCad is the closest thing to a fix, and even it doesn't confirm the fix.** It's the only vendor selling into both the organizer side (ExpoCad) and the GSC side (EXPOCAD Contractor™) [SOURCED]. But its own marketing copy only claims generic "seamless integrations" without describing whether the two products share a live booth database or just a common file format [VENDOR, unverified]. If even the vendor best positioned to have solved this doesn't say so plainly, it's reasonable to conclude it isn't solved cleanly.

4. **The venue is a third silo.** Floor plans get exported as DXF/PDF for "general service contractors" [SOURCED] — the same export path is, by strong industry convention, also how fire-marshal/venue approval typically happens [INDUSTRY-COMMON, not directly confirmed by a fetch]. Momentus/Ungerboeck only closes this gap for the minority of shows where the venue operator is also the organizer (Messe München, Jaarbeurs, MCH Group) [SOURCED]. For the majority case — independent organizer, independent venue, independent GSC — there is no vendor in this research that claims to connect all three.

5. **The exhibitor logs into 3+ systems for one event, each asking for data the others already have:** the show-management exhibitor portal (MYS Exhibitor Resource Center / a2z Event Portal / Cvent Exhibitor Management) for profile, staff badges, directory listing, leads; the GSC ordering portal (Expresso / ExhibitorPro / Freeman Online) for booth services, re-entering company name, booth number, and billing info; and — separately again, per Swapcard's and Grip's product framing (both explicitly position themselves as a plug-in layer, not a system of record) — often a third engagement/matchmaking layer if the organizer licenses one on top of whichever floor-plan platform they use. A fourth system (hotel/housing) is common industry practice for larger shows [INDUSTRY-COMMON, not fetched this session].

6. **Checked directly from the GSC side for the first time, 2026-08-07 — same silence, independently confirmed.** The original research only checked organizer-side platforms; this pass checked GSC-operations platforms directly for any mention of MYS/a2z/ExpoCad integration. **GoShow** [SOURCED, goshowonline.com] — GSC operations platform (freight, inventory, exhibitor portal, self-service kiosk) — no mention. **ConventionSuite** (NewGen) [SOURCED, newgennow.com] — same internal-real-time-ops pattern, no organizer-system connection mentioned. **The Expo Group's own ExpoPortal** [SOURCED, theexpogroup.com/expoportal/] — notable because The Expo Group is itself named on MYS's partners page (see point 2 above) — runs its own ordering portal structurally identical to Freeman's/GES's, with no integration claims in either direction. Three for three, silent.

7. **New nuance, 2026-08-07: MYS has built the technical door, but no evidence anyone's walking through it.** MYS's own FAQ, asked directly "We use another vendor to manage our exhibitor data... can MYS integrate with them?", gives only a generic non-answer: "Map Your Show has integrations already set up with many different trade show vendors. If yours is not one we currently have in place, we can evaluate if we would be able to develop an integration with them" [SOURCED, mapyourshow.com/why-mys/faq.cfm] — no GSC named even when directly prompted. The one confirmed, technically-described consumer of MYS's API is **Swapcard**, and it is explicitly **not** bidirectional and **not** booth-assignment-write: Swapcard's integration page describes a one-way, 60-minute-polling import of "company name, booth number, logo, description, social links, and contact details, from Map Your Show into Swapcard," for display/embed only [SOURCED, swapcard.com/platform/integrations/map-your-show]. But MYS's own REST API (`api.mapyourshow.com` — see Section 1) goes considerably further than what Swapcard consumes: it exposes `/Booths/Assignments` with GET/POST/DELETE, i.e., **write access to booth-to-exhibitor assignment**, explicitly scoped in its v2 docs for "third-party vendors and GSCs." No GSC is confirmed as an approved consumer of that endpoint for order-fulfillment. This changes the shape of the finding: the seam is no longer purely a technical gap for MYS specifically — it's now better described as a **business-adoption gap**, since the plumbing already exists.

**This is the strategic opening — sharpened, not overturned, by the 2026-08-07 pass.** No vendor researched — including the one (ExpoCad) with products on both sides of the seam, and now including MYS despite having built a technically-capable API — publicly demonstrates a live, bidirectional data path from "booth assigned in the floor plan" to "GSC ordering portal already knows the booth number, dimensions, and exhibitor contact." What's changed: for MYS specifically, there is now a documented existing technical door (a booth-assignment read/write API explicitly scoped for GSC consumption) that nobody is confirmed to be using for that purpose. A minimal-surface product that owned that single handoff (push booth assignment + exhibitor identity from the show-management system into GES/Freeman/Shepard's ordering systems, and pull order status back) would remove the one step every exhibitor and every show manager currently does by hand or not at all — and, for MYS at least, might be buildable as an approved integration partner against an API that already exists, rather than requiring net-new plumbing from zero.
