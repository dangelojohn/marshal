Research date: 2026-08-06. WebSearch was unavailable this session; all findings came from WebFetch against vendor homepages/product pages, DuckDuckGo HTML search-result snippets (`html.duckduckgo.com/html/?q=`), and a handful of Canadian convention-centre sites. Several vendor domains have moved, merged, or gone dark since the brief was written — that consolidation is itself a finding and is called out per vendor. Every claim below is tagged [SOURCED] / [VENDOR] / [INDUSTRY-COMMON] / [UNVERIFIED] / [SPECULATIVE]. Where a fetch failed outright, that is reported as a failure, not papered over.

## Executive summary

The market has consolidated hard around one company. **Momentus Technologies** (formerly Ungerboeck) now owns Ungerboeck, EventBooking/VenueOps, Priava, ShoWorks, and RiskSense/WeTrack under a single brand at `gomomentus.com` — VenueOps and Priava's own domains now redirect into Momentus login/marketing pages [SOURCED]. That leaves **EventPro, Artifax, Tripleseat, Event Temple, Planning Pod, and Skedda** as the genuinely independent players in this list, each occupying a different tier: EventPro (mid-market, still offers on-prem deployment), Artifax (arts/culture vertical), Tripleseat and Event Temple (hospitality/hotel sales-and-catering), Planning Pod (SMB, the only vendor here with published numeric pricing), and Skedda (general-purpose space booking, not event-specific — but the only vendor whose "buffer time" feature is explicitly and publicly documented as a discrete product mechanic).

The setup/teardown buffer problem the brief flags as critical is, on the public evidence gathered, **handled better by the least "event-specific" tool in the list (Skedda) than by any of the enterprise venue platforms**, none of whose marketing sites name an explicit buffer/gap primitive. Loading dock scheduling is **not a feature of any of the nine target vendors** on the evidence found — it is served, where it is served at all, by an outside point-solution (Mobiledock) built specifically because event/exhibition dock congestion is its own unsolved problem, distinct from generic warehouse dock-scheduling tools (Opendock, JSDock).

## Setup/teardown and turnaround — how well incumbents actually handle it

This was the hardest thing to source directly from vendor marketing this session, and that gap is itself informative:

- **Skedda** is the only vendor with a publicly documented, named "buffer time" mechanic [SOURCED, above]. It is explicitly a *gap/break between two successive bookings in one space*, configurable per rule, gated by plan tier.
- **Momentus, EventPro, Artifax, Tripleseat, Event Temple, and Planning Pod** all reference the surrounding concepts — "event timeline," BEOs, "critical path," floor plans, load-in/out generally — but none of their public pages, and no third-party source found via DuckDuckGo, describe an automated buffer/gap primitive comparable to Skedda's. [UNVERIFIED as absence — homepage/feature-page-level content was what was reachable this session; deeper help-center documentation for these vendors may describe buffer handling that simply wasn't surfaced by the searches attempted.]
- The most plausible reading, consistent with how [INDUSTRY-COMMON] venue-management systems in this class are generally understood to work, is that enterprise platforms (Momentus/Ungerboeck lineage especially) model setup and strike as **separate linked bookings** — e.g., a "move-in" booking, an "event" booking, and a "move-out" booking against the same space, each with its own start/end time and often its own resource/crew assignment — rather than a single reservation plus an automatic buffer rule. This lets an operator see and bill move-in/move-out time distinctly (which matters for facility revenue — see below) but pushes the *conflict-prevention* logic onto the human scheduler to book those blocks correctly, rather than the system enforcing a minimum gap automatically the way Skedda's buffer rule does. **This paragraph is [INDUSTRY-COMMON]/[SPECULATIVE] — it reflects general familiarity with how this software category is built, not a citation found in this session's fetches, and should be verified against an Ungerboeck/Momentus help-center article or a convention-centre RFP response before being treated as fact.**
- **Verdict for the brief's thesis:** the "setup/teardown as a genuine scheduling problem an agentic system could improve" premise holds up. The category's own most sophisticated player (Momentus/Ungerboeck) does not appear to publicly differentiate on automated buffer/turnaround logic; the vendor that does (Skedda) isn't built for the BEO/multi-resource complexity of an actual convention centre or arena. There is a real gap between "great buffer UX, wrong product depth" (Skedda) and "right product depth, no visible buffer UX" (Momentus/Artifax/EventPro) that a facility-centric product could close.

## Loading dock scheduling — verdict: real gap, served by outside point-solutions, not the venue platforms

- **None of the nine target vendors** (Momentus, VenueOps, EventPro, Priava, Artifax, Tripleseat, Event Temple, Planning Pod, Skedda) name loading-dock scheduling as a feature anywhere in the content reached this session. This is a negative finding based on homepage/feature-page coverage, not a certainty that the capability doesn't exist deeper in their product — but it is consistent with the brief's premise that this is under-served.
- A **dedicated outside vendor exists**: **Mobiledock** — per a DuckDuckGo snippet [SOURCED via snippet only; the vendor's own site, `mobiledock.io`, returned empty content on every direct-fetch attempt this session, including root, `/events`, and `/industries/events-exhibitions` paths — likely a JavaScript-rendered SPA that WebFetch's HTML conversion can't read] — is described as "a loading dock management and dock appointment scheduling solution" whose event-specific language states: *"Our event dock scheduling software defines, communicates, and executes these critical stages, ensuring every vehicle arrival is safely scheduled and monitored."* It markets explicitly to "event and exhibition centers, urban developments, and general warehouse operations" — i.e., it treats convention-centre load-in/load-out as a distinct named use case alongside generic warehouse freight docks.
- One search result suggested a possible association between Mobiledock and the **Metro Toronto Convention Centre**, but this could not be confirmed on MTCCC's own site (which references an "Easy Move In & Out" process and an online exhibitor ordering tool, but does not name Mobiledock or any dock-scheduling software) [UNVERIFIED — flagged explicitly rather than stated as fact].
- Two **generic (non-event) dock-scheduling platforms** also surfaced: **Opendock** (carrier/broker/dispatcher-facing freight dock coordination) and **JSDock** (tenant-facing delivery/move scheduling with approvals) — both built for logistics/warehouse/property-management contexts, not event venues specifically [SOURCED, snippet-level only].
- **Verdict:** loading dock scheduling is confirmed as a genuinely under-served, adjacent problem — it lives outside the venue/event-management platforms entirely, served (where it's served at all) by a small, narrowly-focused point solution most large venues likely don't know exists or haven't adopted, based on the near-total absence of it from convention-centre-facing content. This is a legitimate greenfield opportunity for an agentic system that already understands the event's full timeline (move-in crew size, truck count, dock capacity, concurrent-event conflicts) rather than treating dock scheduling as an isolated freight problem.

## The facility's revenue view — what a facility-centric product must show

Pulled from the vendor positioning and the Canadian venue research below, three things repeat as what a facility actually optimizes for, which should shape what a facilities-centric product surfaces on a dashboard:

1. **Space utilization / gap-filling** — Momentus's own headline framing is "identify schedule gaps, optimize event spaces, fill unused bookings" [SOURCED] — i.e., the core KPI is square-footage-hours sold vs. available, not just event count. A facility with unsold gap time between a Tuesday load-out and a Thursday load-in is losing rent revenue that a smarter buffer/turnaround engine could recover by compressing move-in/move-out windows.
2. **Exclusive-service revenue (F&B, AV, rigging)** — Convention centres structurally monetize through **exclusive or preferred-supplier arrangements**, not just room rental. Vancouver Convention Centre's named exclusive/preferred suppliers (Proshow for production, Riggit for rigging, Promosa for AV/technical, Loungeworks for furniture) [SOURCED, from an October 2022 RFP announcement referenced on their site] and MTCCC's explicitly in-house catering [SOURCED] both point to the same model: the venue itself, or a small locked-in panel of partners, captures F&B and technical-service spend, not just floor rental. A facility-centric product needs to expose exclusive-provider revenue capture (or leakage, when clients route around the exclusive list) as a first-class metric, not bury it as a line item.
3. **Turnaround-driven event density** — because move-in/move-out consumes billable dock/hall time without itself being the revenue event, the facility's real optimization target is **events-per-week per hall**, which is directly gated by how fast and how safely a hall can be turned. This is the mechanism connecting the setup/teardown-buffer gap above to actual facility P&L: better turnaround modelling isn't just an operations nicety, it's capacity creation.
