## Setup/teardown and turnaround — how well incumbents actually handle it

This was the hardest thing to source directly from vendor marketing this session, and that gap is itself informative:

- **Skedda** is the only vendor with a publicly documented, named "buffer time" mechanic [SOURCED, above]. It is explicitly a *gap/break between two successive bookings in one space*, configurable per rule, gated by plan tier.
- **Momentus, EventPro, Artifax, Tripleseat, Event Temple, and Planning Pod** all reference the surrounding concepts — "event timeline," BEOs, "critical path," floor plans, load-in/out generally — but none of their public pages, and no third-party source found via DuckDuckGo, describe an automated buffer/gap primitive comparable to Skedda's. [UNVERIFIED as absence — homepage/feature-page-level content was what was reachable this session; deeper help-center documentation for these vendors may describe buffer handling that simply wasn't surfaced by the searches attempted.]
- **CONFIRMED 2026-08-07, upgraded from [SPECULATIVE] to [SOURCED], with the precise mechanic name**: enterprise platforms (Momentus/Ungerboeck specifically) model setup and strike as **separate linked bookings**, exactly as this paragraph originally guessed — the mechanic is called **"Space Usage."** A booked space carries a usage tag; the four types are **Event, Move In, Move Out, and Dark Day**. "A single event will typically be divided into load-in days, event days, and load-out days" [elitesupportcenter.ungerboeck.com/hc/en-us/articles/17437531506967-Space-Usages]. Usage "is a higher-level designation that allows you to divide the event, without getting as granular as the function schedule," and usage dates "can also be used in contracts to show in and out dates which are separate from the event start and end dates." Per "How are events structured in Momentus Elite?": each booked space is a single room for a single day; multi-day bookings get one booked-space record per day, each independently taggable with a usage. Momentus does have conflict-checking/color-coded double-booking warnings, but — checked specifically for "buffer"/"turnaround time"/"gap" in Momentus's own docs — **no evidence of an automatic minimum-gap rule comparable to Skedda's**. This *strengthens* the file's original thesis rather than softening it: the confirmed mechanism is a human manually tagging Move In/Move Out records, with conflict-detection as a safety net, not an enforced buffer — a real, now-precisely-citable gap in automation exactly where an agentic layer could add value.
- **Verdict for the brief's thesis:** the "setup/teardown as a genuine scheduling problem an agentic system could improve" premise holds up. The category's own most sophisticated player (Momentus/Ungerboeck) does not appear to publicly differentiate on automated buffer/turnaround logic; the vendor that does (Skedda) isn't built for the BEO/multi-resource complexity of an actual convention centre or arena. There is a real gap between "great buffer UX, wrong product depth" (Skedda) and "right product depth, no visible buffer UX" (Momentus/Artifax/EventPro) that a facility-centric product could close.

## The facility's revenue view — what a facility-centric product must show

Pulled from the vendor positioning and the Canadian venue research below, three things repeat as what a facility actually optimizes for, which should shape what a facilities-centric product surfaces on a dashboard:

1. **Space utilization / gap-filling** — Momentus's own headline framing is "identify schedule gaps, optimize event spaces, fill unused bookings" [SOURCED] — i.e., the core KPI is square-footage-hours sold vs. available, not just event count. A facility with unsold gap time between a Tuesday load-out and a Thursday load-in is losing rent revenue that a smarter buffer/turnaround engine could recover by compressing move-in/move-out windows.
2. **Exclusive-service revenue (F&B, AV, rigging)** — Convention centres structurally monetize through **exclusive or preferred-supplier arrangements**, not just room rental. Vancouver Convention Centre's named exclusive/preferred suppliers (Proshow for production, Riggit for rigging, Promosa for AV/technical, Loungeworks for furniture) [SOURCED, from an October 2022 RFP announcement referenced on their site] and MTCCC's explicitly in-house catering [SOURCED] both point to the same model: the venue itself, or a small locked-in panel of partners, captures F&B and technical-service spend, not just floor rental. A facility-centric product needs to expose exclusive-provider revenue capture (or leakage, when clients route around the exclusive list) as a first-class metric, not bury it as a line item.
3. **Turnaround-driven event density** — because move-in/move-out consumes billable dock/hall time without itself being the revenue event, the facility's real optimization target is **events-per-week per hall**, which is directly gated by how fast and how safely a hall can be turned. This is the mechanism connecting the setup/teardown-buffer gap above to actual facility P&L: better turnaround modelling isn't just an operations nicety, it's capacity creation.
