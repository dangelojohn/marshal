## 0. ⚠ Headline finding (2026-08-07): a direct competitor already holds Canada's three biggest venues

**Voyage Control** (`voyagecontrol.com`) is dock-appointment-scheduling software already deployed, by name, at:
- **Metro Toronto Convention Centre** [SOURCED — MTCC's own "Loading Dock Management System" PDF]: *"introduced a loading dock management system for booking a loading dock time powered by Voyage Control."* MTCC was reportedly the first convention centre in North America to use it. Booking portal: `voyagecontrol.com/mtcc`.
- **Vancouver Convention Centre** [SOURCED, corroborated across multiple independent sources]: in use since FanExpo Vancouver 2017, now "part of the Convention Centre's suite of services offered to all clients with over 100 staff trained to use it."
- **BMO Centre, Calgary** [SOURCED, mullerexpo.com]: *"access to the loading dock apron is contingent on having a confirmed booking via Voyage Control."*

No confirmation of Voyage Control at RBC Winnipeg, Quebec City CC, Edmonton EXPO, or Halifax — apparently unserved, or at least undocumented.

**Company background** [SOURCED, aggregated from LeadIQ/Tracxn/CBInsights]: founded **2009 in London, UK** by James Swanston. Its **primary business is construction-site logistics** — homepage customers are all construction firms (Suffolk, Gilbane, Lendlease, Skanska, Jacobs, Mortenson, Clark, HITT). Events/venues read as a secondary vertical bolted onto a construction-logistics core, not an events-native product. **Thinly funded** — Crunchbase/Tracxn show only **~$190K total raised** (CEMEX Ventures, Taronga Ventures; latest round a $140K convertible note, Jan 2022).

**[SPECULATIVE]**: entrenched-but-thin is a specific, exploitable shape — real incumbency at exactly the venues that matter most, but a construction-logistics product adapted to events rather than built for multi-exhibitor GSC-coordinated marshalling, and a competitor too undercapitalized to have iterated hard on the event-specific case. This changes §6's verdict below: in the *dock-scheduling-software* layer specifically, "no player has a Canada-wide lock" is no longer true — it needs to be read as "no lock in GSC/labour services," with a real, if beatable, incumbent in the scheduling layer itself.

**Two other dock-scheduling vendors exist, neither active in Canada**: **Mobiledock** (UK) markets specifically to "Events & Exhibition Centres" (Harrogate, Glasgow SEC, and Australian venues) — the closest thing to a purpose-built direct competitor, but has not entered Canada. **Veyor** (Australia) covers malls/offices/data-centers/event-venues broadly (JLL, Westfield, CBRE as customers) with a Texas office but no Canadian presence found.

**A Canadian dock-scheduling company exists but hasn't crossed into events**: **DataDocks** (`datadocks.com`), Bolton, Ontario, founded 2013/2014, bootstrapped, exclusively warehouse/distribution-center dock scheduling — confirmed via direct fetch to have no event-venue mention anywhere on its site. Real evidence the warehouse-dock and event-dock categories remain separate markets in Canada today, with nobody straddling them — a potential partner, acquihire target, or reference architecture, not a current competitor.

## 1. Canadian players — who's actually there

- **Stronco** [SOURCED, VENDOR] — Canadian GSC, "over 70 years" in the trade show industry, Ontario-rooted ("A Made in Ontario Story"). Confirmed as the **official/exclusive show services provider at Cohere Centre, Ottawa** (formerly EY Centre) via its own announcement. Services: exhibitions, events, exhibits, creative, logistics.
- **GES Canada** [SOURCED] — has a Toronto office (recently announced a new GM there) and a confirmed **partnership with Rogers Centre Ottawa** (formerly Shaw Centre) — i.e., a *different* GSC than Stronco holds the *other* major Ottawa venue. Active on CPMA 2026 and PDAC. GES's own site (`ges.com/ca/`) is the working Canada portal.
- **Showtech Power & Lighting** [SOURCED, VENDOR] — Canada-wide, but a narrower service layer: electrical, lighting, sign/banner hanging, mechanical. Claims exclusive supplier status "at several major conference venues across Canada" — names not disclosed on its homepage.
- **Levy Show Service** [SOURCED, VENDOR] — full-service GSC (design, exhibits, transportation/customs, floor management, labour, cleaning, AV/rigging) with a dedicated **Canada office in Richmond, BC**; cited safety-partnership work with Vancouver Convention Centre. Reads as a Western Canada regional player.
- **Freeman** [UNVERIFIED] — could not confirm any Canadian presence this session. `freeman.com/locations/canada/` and `where-we-are/` both 404'd; the homepage content fetched contained no Canada reference. This is a fetch-failure gap, not proof Freeman has no Canadian operations — worth a follow-up pass with different URLs.
- **Vancouver Convention Centre's own exclusive-supplier list** (Oct 2022 announcement) [SOURCED] names **Proshow, Riggit, Promosa, and Loungeworks** — none of the "big" national GSC names. This is a real signal: BC's flagship venue runs on regional specialty suppliers, not Freeman/GES/Stronco.
- **Global Convention Services Ltd.** [SOURCED 2026-08-07, multiple independent listings] — a genuine miss from the first pass. **Headquartered in Saint John, New Brunswick**, founded **1989** (35+ years), full-service GSC (pipe & drape, Octanorm hardwall, graphics, warehousing/trucks/labour, install). Runs fully staffed branches in **Vancouver, Victoria, Calgary, Saint John, Moncton, Halifax, and Charlottetown** — a deliberate Western+Atlantic footprint with **no Ontario or Quebec branch**, where Stronco/GES/LiUNA already dominate. Structurally interesting: the market appears to split into an Ontario/Quebec corridor and a Western+Atlantic corridor, not a single national pattern.
- **Encore Canada** [VENDOR + cross-referenced 2026-08-07 across three separate venue pages] — confirmed this pass as the exclusive or official AV/staging/rigging provider at **BMO Centre Calgary, RBC Convention Centre Winnipeg, and Quebec City Convention Centre** — three venues, three provinces, independently. A real national-consolidation signal in the AV/staging layer specifically, distinct from the fragmented GSC layer. Self-describes as "Canadian owned," though it operates under the global Encore/PSAV brand family — that claim is un-probed further.

**Market structure verdict:** fragmented, not consolidated, in general service contracting and labour. Ottawa alone has two major venues with two *different* exclusive GSCs (Stronco at Cohere/EY, GES at Rogers/Shaw). Vancouver's exclusive roster is entirely regional specialists. No player found holds Canada-wide exclusive lock in GSC services the way Freeman/GES dominate large swaths of the US. [SPECULATIVE, drawn from the sourced facts above] **This does NOT hold in the AV layer (Encore, cross-provincial) or the dock-scheduling-software layer (Voyage Control, at the three biggest venues) — see §0.**

## 3. Canada-specific structural facts

**Bilingual / Bill 96** [SOURCED — Wikipedia + CAI/LégisQuébec redirects confirm the framework]: Extends the Charter of the French Language to businesses with **25–49 employees** (previously 50+ threshold). Software (incl. UIs) **must be available in French unless no French version exists**, deadline **June 2025** (already past). Same June 2025 deadline applied to product packaging/labels/instructions. Commercial signage must have French "markedly predominant." Employers must communicate with employees in French first. Contracts of adhesion default to French. Provision requiring businesses to pay for certified French translation of legal documents was judicially challenged (suspended pending litigation as of Aug 2022) — current status not re-verified this session. Bill 96 also invoked the notwithstanding clause. **This is not a nicety — a Canada-facing SaaS selling into Quebec today is already past the compliance deadline for a French UI.**

**Privacy / Law 25** [SOURCED — direct from CAI, Quebec's privacy regulator]: Staggered rollout — breach notification + regulator registry + administrative penalties (up to **2% of global revenue or $10M CAD**) effective **Sept 2022**; privacy officer designation, new consent standard ("manifeste, libre, éclairé"), and — critically for a SaaS platform — **mandatory privacy impact assessment before transferring personal information outside Quebec**, effective **Sept 2023**; biometric database use requires 60-day advance notice to the regulator. A US-hosted platform selling to Quebec venues/organizers carries a real compliance step (the cross-border PIA) that Canadian-hosted competitors don't.

**PIPEDA** [SOURCED — OPC]: Federal baseline (10 fair-information principles: consent, purpose limitation, accuracy, safeguards, access, accountability). Provinces with "substantially similar" laws (Quebec's Law 25, and similar in BC/Alberta) are generally understood to displace PIPEDA for intra-provincial activity [INDUSTRY-COMMON — not explicitly confirmed on the fetched OPC page].

**Tax (GST/HST/PST/QST)** [SOURCED — rate table]: Five different regimes across the country: GST-only 5% (Alberta, territories), GST+PST (BC 12%, Saskatchewan 11%, Manitoba 12%), HST (Ontario 13%, NB/NL/PEI 15%, **Nova Scotia 14% — reduced from 15% effective April 1, 2025**), and GST+QST in Quebec (14.975% combined). Place-of-supply rules for services specifically **could not be sourced** this session (CRA page 403'd) — flagged as [UNVERIFIED]; general principle that event/venue services are taxed in the province where delivered is [INDUSTRY-COMMON], not confirmed against CRA text.

**Customs / cross-border** [SOURCED — Canadian Chamber of Commerce, the actual issuing body]: **ATA Carnets** are the standard mechanism — the Canadian Chamber of Commerce is Canada's **exclusive national guaranteeing/issuing body** (since 1972), enabling duty/tax-free temporary import (1-year validity) of trade show displays, commercial samples, and professional equipment into 80+ countries, covering essentially all goods except consumables/disposables and repair items. This is the real, genuine pain point flagged in the brief: US exhibitors bringing booth materials into Canada (and vice versa) either use a carnet or face full duty/tax exposure and CBSA temporary-importation paperwork. Today this friction is absorbed as a **service** by GSCs/brokers (Levy Show Service explicitly lists "transportation and customs management" as a line item), not solved by software — worth noting for anyone assuming this is a clean software wedge.

**Accessibility / AODA** [SOURCED — ontario.ca]: Applies to Ontario businesses/nonprofits with **50+ employees**; public-facing websites/content published after Jan 1, 2012 must meet **WCAG 2.0 Level AA**; compliance deadline was **Jan 1, 2021** (already past — no grace period remaining). Internal-only sites (intranet/extranet) are exempt.

**Currency** [INDUSTRY-COMMON, not sourced this session]: CAD is standard; cross-border shows commonly quote US exhibitors in USD. Not independently verified this session.

**Labour/unions** — **partially resolved 2026-08-07, Toronto only.** [SOURCED — read directly from the primary collective-agreement PDFs, not summaries]: at **Exhibition Place, Toronto**, **LiUNA Local 506** holds jurisdiction over *"all material handling; cleaning; erection, dismantling, decorating and setting up of trade and consumer shows, exhibits and displays"* (Letter of Intent #4 of its CBA with the Board of Governors of Exhibition Place, `explace.on.ca`). Exhibition Place's stated general policy is to operate as a unionized LiUNA site, and trade-show leases must require jurisdiction-matching work go to Local 506-bound companies. LiUNA 506's own site claims its ~500 members also cover MTCC — that extension is **[VENDOR-claimed, not independently contract-verified]**. Separately, **IATSE Local 58**'s own CBA with Exhibition Place is narrow and explicit: stagehand/theatrical/Technical Services Shop only, with a clause stating no jurisdiction not expressly listed may be inferred — i.e. **IATSE does not hold exhibit-floor material handling in Toronto; that's LiUNA's alone.** Toronto is therefore a clean two-craft split, genuinely simpler than several major US venues (e.g. the EACA's own jurisdiction reference lists six separate crafts at Chicago's McCormick Place/Navy Pier).

**Still unresolved outside Toronto** [UNVERIFIED]: Vancouver, Montreal, Calgary, Winnipeg, Quebec City, Edmonton, Halifax — no exhibit-floor union jurisdiction confirmed for any of them. Found only generic, non-venue-specific union presence (UNITE HERE Local 40 in Vancouver = banquet/F&B, not exhibit floor; Teamsters Local 213 exists in BC generally; Teamsters Local 106 represents ~5,500 truck drivers in Quebec generally) — none confirm venue-specific exhibit-floor jurisdiction. No exhibitor manual for Calgary/Winnipeg/Quebec City/Edmonton/Halifax mentions any union labour requirement at all, which could mean genuinely open-shop operation or could just mean it isn't publicized — **cannot distinguish which, and this should not be read as evidence of either.**

**A genuinely informative negative finding**: the EACA's own 19-page "Guide to Union Jurisdictions by City" PDF — the canonical US industry reference — **contains zero Canadian cities.** So Canada isn't merely under-searched by us; it isn't tracked by the standard US reference either. **Revised verdict**: the original blanket "Canadian venues have less jurisdictional complexity" claim is now sourced-true for Toronto specifically, but should **not** be generalized nationally on that single data point.

## 4. Associations & show organisers

- The `cemaonline.com` domain given in the brief is **not** a Canadian exhibition association — it resolves to the **Corporate Event Marketing Association**, a US-based community for event marketers [SOURCED — correction to the brief's assumption]. I could not locate a live, fetchable "Canadian Exhibition Management Association" site this session — flag as **[UNVERIFIED]**, not confirmed to exist under that name/domain.
- **RESOLVED 2026-08-07 — it's the CAEM, not "CEMA."** **Canadian Association of Exposition Management** (`caem.ca`) [SOURCED] self-describes as *"the only national association solely dedicated to promoting, supporting, and defending the interests of the trade and consumer show industry in Canada."* Three membership tiers: show producers/planners/managers, suppliers, and venues. Formed mid-1970s, incorporated 1983, renamed CAEM in 2000. Toronto-based; management outsourced to Essentient Association Management since Jan 2024. Runs a CEM (Certified in Exhibition Management) designation and an annual conference (2026: June 15–17, Windsor ON). Partners with IAEE, Meetings Mean Business Canada, and UFI. **Has a Suppliers/Associates Directory** (`caem.ca/membership/suppliers-directory/`) that is almost certainly the fastest path to a real regional-vendor census — but it's a JS-rendered/member-gated search widget, not fetchable by WebFetch/WebSearch. **Worth a manual or browser-automation follow-up** if a fuller vendor roster is ever needed.
- **APEQ — Association des professionnels en exposition au Québec** (`apeq.org`) [SOURCED] — Quebec-*provincial* equivalent of CAEM, "since 1991," covering promoters, suppliers, and venue renters, maintains its own Quebec trade-show calendar.
- **Convention Centres Canada (CCC)** (`conventioncentrescanada.com`) [SOURCED] — a third, distinct association, venue-side only (CEOs/senior leaders of convention *centres*, not organizers or suppliers). Mission: "advance the profile, professionalism and business opportunities for member convention centres." Full member list gated behind a "Find a Convention Centre" tool; Enercare Centre (Toronto) and St. John's Convention Centre confirmed as members via photo credits, so its reach includes Atlantic Canada.
- **IAEE** confirmed it has **no Canadian chapters** — only 12 US regional chapters listed on its own chapters page [SOURCED].
- **CMEexpo** (Canadian Meetings + Events Expo) [SOURCED] — a genuine, long-running (34 years) Canadian trade show specifically for meeting/event planners and suppliers; 2026 edition Aug 11–12. This is a real go-to-market venue for a Canadian platform, not a market-structure data source.

## 5. Market sizing

**Substantially resolved 2026-08-07 — with a scope caveat that matters.** The prior pass found nothing; this pass found two generations of a real, named, methodologically-documented figure. But both measure **"business events"** (meetings + conferences + conventions + incentive travel + trade shows, combined) — **not** trade shows/exhibitions isolated the way CEIR isolates them for the US. Keep that distinction; don't let the headline number silently stand in for "trade show industry size."

- **Baseline — CEIS 3.0** [SOURCED, cited by MPI Toronto Chapter], "Economic Contribution of Business Events in Canada," commissioned 2013 by MPI Foundation Canada + Conference Board of Canada + Maritz Research + Greenfield Services, using **2012 data**: 35.3M participants, **$29.0B direct spending**, **$27.5B GDP contribution (~1.5% of Canada's total GDP)**, $8.5B in taxes/fees to government, 200,000+ full-year direct jobs, **585,000+ business events held in 2012 across 2,000+ venues**.
- **Current — Destination Canada's own claim** [SOURCED, destinationcanada.com — a federal Crown corporation's own tourism-marketing claim, so treat with the same caution as a VENDOR tag]: business events (explicitly incl. trade shows) contribute **"$47 billion to Canada's economy" annually**, support **"more than 240,000 jobs."** Secondary, unverified figure: ~2,555 business events in 2024 with ~1.2M delegates (~51K international overnight).
- **A more rigorous study is in progress, not yet published**: the **Canadian Business Events Economic Impact Study (CBEEIS)**, commissioned by Destination Canada + Meetings Mean Business Canada, contracted to the Conference Board of Canada in 2025, examining 15–16 major events held 2018–2024. A related "Legacy & Impact Study" exists at `businessevents.destinationcanada.com/en-ca/legacy` but its numeric detail lives in a PDF not extracted this session — **[UNVERIFIED]**, worth pulling directly if a harder number is needed later.
- **NAICS code confirmed**: **561920, "Convention and trade show organizers"** [SOURCED]. Could **not** retrieve a live StatCan revenue figure for it — the ISED "Canadian Industry Statistics" page has migrated to a JS search app (`ised-isde.canada.ca/app/ixb/cis/search-recherche`) that WebFetch can't render. **[UNVERIFIED — a technical fetch failure, not a confirmed absence]**; StatCan likely has a real figure behind that interface, worth a manual query.

**Bottom line:** the absolute-zero finding from the original pass no longer holds — real, sourced figures exist ($27.5–47B GDP, 200–240K jobs, depending on study vintage). The narrower gap — a number isolating trade shows/exhibitions the way CEIR does for the US — remains technically open.

## 6. Verdict: is Canada-first a real structural opening?

**Yes, but as a beachhead, not a standalone thesis.** Two genuinely defensible, sourced advantages: (1) Bill 96's software-French deadline has *already passed* (June 2025) and Law 25's cross-border-transfer PIA requirement is real and currently a live compliance cost for any US-hosted competitor selling into Quebec — a bilingual, Canadian-hosted platform removes friction competitors carry today, not friction that might someday matter. (2) The market is provably fragmented, not Freeman/GES-locked — Ottawa's two flagship venues run on two different exclusive GSCs, and Vancouver's exclusive roster skips the national names entirely for regional specialists. That means no single incumbent has to be dislodged; the wedge is winning trust with venues/organizers that a US-headquartered platform treats as an afterthought.

**But the honest counterweight is real too:** the total market ceiling is now roughly sourced ($27.5–47B business-events GDP, though not exhibition-specific — see §5), and the number of primary venues worth targeting nationally is roughly a dozen. GES already has a Toronto beachhead and an Ottawa venue relationship, so the space isn't virgin. And the sharpest "Canada pain point" in the brief — customs/carnets — is currently solved as a *service* by existing GSCs (Levy explicitly sells it), not as an acute unmet software gap, so a platform still has to prove it beats what a competent local GSC already bundles into its fee.

**⚠ Revised 2026-08-07 — the counterweight that matters most isn't regulatory, it's competitive.** §0 above is the material update: **Voyage Control already runs dock scheduling at MTCC, Vancouver Convention Centre, and BMO Calgary — three of the handful of venues that would anchor a Canadian go-to-market.** That doesn't kill the thesis — Voyage Control is a thinly-funded (~$190K raised) construction-logistics product with events bolted on, not a purpose-built multi-exhibitor GSC-coordinated marshalling tool, and Marshal's chokepoint/buffer model (D-014) is a genuinely different and more sophisticated mechanism than appointment slots. But it means **Q-58 and the two OPEN_ITEMS phone calls now need a third question**: does the venue ops director you call already use Voyage Control, and if so, what specifically is it failing to do for them? That answer determines whether Marshal's pitch at Toronto/Vancouver/Calgary is "replace this" (harder, needs a real wedge) or "we're additive/different" — versus RBC Winnipeg, Quebec City, Edmonton, and Halifax, which are apparently still open.

Read the overall picture as: real, defensible niche for bilingual + compliance + fragmented mid-market fit, **but the three biggest venues are not a clean greenfield** — size the beachhead expecting to either displace or coexist with an incumbent at exactly the venues with the most volume, and validate that before assuming Toronto/Vancouver/Calgary are first-sale targets.
