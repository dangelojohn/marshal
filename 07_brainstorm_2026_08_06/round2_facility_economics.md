## 🔴 Correction 1 — insurance is oversold if pitched as premium reduction

- GL/umbrella underwriting is **dominated by loss history**. A new control earns
  credit **on a 2–3 year lag**
- Realistic: **$0–10K year 1 (0–4%)**, rising to $10–25K (4–10%) after 2–3 years
  of demonstrated loss experience
- **⚠ It can cut the other way.** If the system records near-misses or hazardous
  conditions the facility then fails to act on, plaintiff's counsel gets a
  *"you had the data and didn't act"* heightened-duty-of-care argument. **A real
  double-edged sword, not just upside**

> **Never lead with "cuts your insurance." Lead with "makes your claims cheaper
> to fight and easier to win"** — defensible on day one, no lag, no downside.

## 🔴 Correction 2 — I cited the wrong comparable

I used hotel OTAs (15–25%) to justify 15%. **Wrong reasoning even though the
number is close.** OTA commission is priced substantially for **bringing the
customer**. Marshal brings no exhibitors — **the trucks are coming regardless.**

**The right anchor is "a fair split of newly created value for real operational
work performed," not a finder's fee for new demand.** Parking-reservation
marketplaces and ticketing service fees remain the correct structural analogues:
monetising idle physical capacity via a scheduling layer the owner didn't build.

## Recommended structure — better than my flat 15%

**Band 15–25%. Sweet spot 18–20%.** Three components:

1. **Headline 18–20%** of dock-fee volume
2. **⭐ Per-booking floor of $15–20** — so unit economics don't collapse when a
   venue discounts off-peak slots. *I had missed this entirely: our own
   recommendation to discount off-peak to flatten the peak would have eroded the
   take with no floor.*
3. **Volume-decay tier** — drop to ~15% above ~$1.5M/yr at a venue, so the
   largest venues keep getting an obviously good deal as they scale

### Objection thresholds
- **≤20–22%** — reads as an obvious yes against anchors a GM already knows
  (exclusive service-contractor commissions back to the venue commonly run 10–20%)
- **~25%** — *"why am I giving away a quarter of a revenue line on MY dock,
  forever, to a vendor I could replace?"* Invites a competing bid or a build-it-
  ourselves reflex
- **>30–35%** — reads as a landlord on the facility's own asset. Real ceiling

## Damage recovery — a legitimate upsell, with a governance caveat

Agent's proposal: **a contingency fee of 20–30% on amounts actually billed back
and collected** using Marshal's evidence. Precedented — subrogation firms work at
20–35% contingency — and it only pays on real recovered cash.

**⚠ My flag: this sits in tension with our own principle that Marshal never
profits when the floor goes wrong.** Recovery revenue scales with damage
occurring. It is *weaker* than the penalty problem (Marshal profits from
*recovery*, not from *damage*), but it muddies a story whose whole strength is
cleanliness. **Recommend: keep it out of the core model. Revisit once the base
business is proven.**

Agreed with the agent: **no percentage of the damage-savings bucket.** It's an
avoided cost, not a transaction — nothing to meter, impossible to audit
(*"prove it would have happened without you"*), and it reads as scope creep.

## Two qualifications I should carry forward

**Faster turnaround is worth $0 at a venue with open dates.** The
incremental-show-day line only exists if the venue is **demand-constrained**.
Add to the qualification test alongside the $800K volume threshold.

**The relationship risk isn't in the P&L.** If show organisers or GSCs perceive
the dock fee as antagonising the events that fill the calendar, the facility's
score drops below 6 **regardless of pricing**. That is a stakeholder problem, not
a pricing problem — which is what round 2's other two agents are examining.

## Facility score: 8/10 at 15–20%

At 18%: Marshal $162K · facility keeps $738K + ~$161K other ≈ **$899K net new
value against a one-time $22K fee.** Payback on implementation in under two weeks

**Not scored 10** — deliberately. A deal this lopsided invites the two questions

### The sharpest objection to prepare for
> *"Are we building a permanent toll booth on our own dock that we can never take
> back in-house?"*

Perpetual revenue share against a one-time-fee framing that makes the pitch sound
cheap. **Answer: contractually cap the take rate with no unilateral escalation,
run a paid single-show pilot with real measurement before any multi-year term,
and build the case on the dock-fee line alone so nothing rests on GUESS-level
numbers.**
